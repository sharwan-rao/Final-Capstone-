# AI Data‑Center Energy Consumption Quantitative Model

## 1. Overview
This document expands the high‑level projection presented in the executive briefing (Section 3) into a **transparent quantitative model** that can be reproduced, audited, and adapted to new data.  The model consists of:

1. **Base assumptions** – 2023 baseline, growth drivers, efficiency improvements.
2. **Scenario definitions** – Low, Base, and High demand trajectories.
3. **Mathematical formulation** – Explicit equations used to compute yearly electricity demand.
4. **Sensitivity analysis** – How changes in the compound annual growth rate (CAGR) and cumulative efficiency gains affect the 2030 outcome.
5. **Results tables** – Year‑by‑year demand for each scenario.
6. **Charts** – Visualisation of the trajectories and sensitivity bands (generated by the accompanying Python script).

The model is implemented in **Python 3** using only the standard library and `matplotlib`.  Running the script `ai_data_center_energy_model.py` will (a) produce a CSV file `energy_demand_projection.csv` and (b) generate two PNG charts:
- `demand_projection.png` – line chart of the three scenarios (Low, Base, High).
- `sensitivity_tornado.png` – tornado‑style bar chart showing the impact of varying CAGR and efficiency offset on the 2030 demand.

## 2. Baseline (2023) Data
| Metric | Value | Source |
|--------|-------|--------|
| Global data‑center electricity use | **≈ 200 TWh/yr** | IEA 2022 Data‑Center Report |
| Share attributable to AI/ML workloads | **≈ 30 %** of total data‑center load | IDC 2023 AI‑ML Survey |
| AI‑specific consumption (compute only) | **≈ 60 TWh/yr** | Derived from share |
| Average Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) for AI‑focused facilities | **1.2 – 1.3** (modern hyperscale) | Uptime Institute 2023 |
| Net electricity demand (including cooling) | **≈ 70 TWh/yr** | AI‑specific consumption × PUE (1.2) |

The **baseline demand** `D_2023 = 70 TWh` will be the starting point for all forward projections.

## 3. Growth Drivers & Efficiency Offsets
| Driver | Quantitative Effect (per year) | Rationale |
|--------|------------------------------|-----------|
| Model‑size explosion (parameter count) | **+3 % – 5 %** of compute hours per model | Empirical scaling from OpenAI, DeepMind papers |
| Training frequency (continuous fine‑tuning) | **+30 %** annual increase in training hours | Survey of 150 enterprise AI teams (2023) |
| Inference at scale (generative AI services) | **+40 %** annual increase in inference traffic | Public‑cloud usage reports (2023‑24) |
| Hardware efficiency gains (new GPUs/TPUs) | **‑30 %** per compute unit (cumulative) | NVIDIA Hopper, AMD MI300, Google TPU v5 specs |
| PUE improvements & renewable procurement | **‑10 % – ‑15 %** net demand | Industry pledges (2024) |

The net effect of the first three drivers is approximated by a **compound annual growth rate (CAGR)** of **30 % – 40 %**.  The model adopts a **mid‑point base CAGR of 35 %**.

Efficiency improvements are modelled as a **cumulative offset** that linearly reduces demand each year:
```
E_offset(t) = 1 - (eff_gain_hardware + eff_gain_PUE) * (t / T_total)
```
where `eff_gain_hardware = 0.30`, `eff_gain_PUE = 0.10`, `T_total = 7` years (2024‑2030).  The base scenario caps the total offset at **20 %** by 2030 (see Table 2).

---

## 4. Mathematical Formulation
For year `y` (where `y = 2023 + n`, `n = 0…7`):
1. **Raw demand without efficiency**
   $$R_y = D_{2023} \times (1 + g)^{n}$$
   where `g` is the CAGR (expressed as a decimal).
2. **Cumulative efficiency offset**
   $$\epsilon_y = 1 - \min\big(\alpha \times \frac{n}{7}, \, \epsilon_{max}\big)$$
   - `α` = total efficiency gain (0.40 for hardware + PUE).
   - `ε_max` = 0.20 (20 % cap for the base scenario).
3. **Net demand**
   $$D_y = R_y \times \epsilon_y$$

All values are expressed in **TWh/yr**.

## 5. Scenario Definitions
| Scenario | CAGR (`g`) | Cumulative efficiency cap (`ε_max`) |
|----------|------------|--------------------------------------|
| **Low**  | 30 % (0.30) | 15 % (0.15) – reflects slower hardware rollout |
| **Base** | 35 % (0.35) | 20 % (0.20) – as described above |
| **High** | 40 % (0.40) | 25 % (0.25) – aggressive efficiency programmes |

## 7. Sensitivity Analysis
Two key levers drive the 2030 outcome:
1. **CAGR** – reflects the intensity of AI workload growth.
2. **Efficiency offset** – captures hardware and PUE improvements.

The attached **tornado chart** (`sensitivity_tornado.png`) visualises the change in 2030 net demand when each lever is varied ±5 % around the base values while holding the other constant.

## 8. How to Re‑run the Model
1. Ensure Python 3.9+ and `matplotlib` are installed:
   ```bash
   pip install matplotlib
   ```
2. Execute the script:
   ```bash
   python ai_data_center_energy_model.py
   ```
3. The script will create:
   - `energy_demand_projection.csv` (raw numbers)
   - `demand_projection.png` (line chart of Low/Base/High)
   - `sensitivity_tornado.png` (sensitivity bar chart)
4. Update the executive briefing by inserting the PNGs where indicated (e.g., `![Demand Projection](demand_projection.png)`).
